## The setup is a single sentence

A student council president wants to confess before he graduates. That's it. *Love is Blind* (恋は盲目) has no flashbacks, no love triangle and no backstory. Ibuki has decided, and we're about to watch the universe test that decision.

At 31 pages it is short, yet by the standards of its own judges it is *long*. The Crown award judge, Takeshi Konomi, said it could be done in half the pages. Fujimoto's afterword jokes about the same note from his editors. I think they were technically right and comedically wrong.

## Escalation as structure

The story is built from three interruptions, each bigger than the last: a school errand, a robbery in the rain, an alien invasion. The joke isn't that these things happen. The joke is the **ranking**. Ibuki weighs each disaster and finds it lighter than the sentence he hasn't said yet.

<SpeechBubble cite="Ibuki, VIZ edition (as quoted in reviews)">
  “…that's the least of my priorities!”
</SpeechBubble>

Repetition is the secret. By the third time Ibuki shrugs off a catastrophe, you're no longer laughing at the catastrophe. You're laughing *with* him, because you've accepted his logic. That's when the story can land its payoff.

## The payoff

<Spoiler lang="en" title="The confession and the ending">

The confession doesn't arrive as a planned speech. It slips out mid-rant at the alien, and Yuri simply asks who he means. "To you." After all the noise, the quietest beat in the book is the one that matters. Reports of the ending say the sincerity disarms everyone, the robber and the would-be invaders included. Anitay called the confession superbly executed, and I agree. It works because the story has spent every page making that one line expensive.

</Spoiler>

## What doesn't quite work

- **Yuri is thin.** She's defined almost entirely by her loyalty ("Osu!") and her reaction to Ibuki. That suits a gag one-shot, but it makes the romance one-sided on the page.
- **The middle sags.** The judges' page-count note isn't wrong. A tighter version would hit harder in places.
- **Rough edges.** This is a debut by a 21-year-old art student. If you come from the polish of *Look Back*, expect a sketchier, more frantic hand.

## Verdict

*Love is Blind* is the most purely **fun** story in *17-21*. It's a sincere little romance wearing an apocalypse as a costume. The Comics Journal called it probably the best-written piece in the book. I'd go further: it's the clearest proof that Fujimoto's trademark collision of the tiny and the cosmic was there from page one.

**Read it if:** you like Kaguya-sama-style romantic stubbornness, Toriyama-style short-story gags, or want to see where Chainsaw Man's author started.
