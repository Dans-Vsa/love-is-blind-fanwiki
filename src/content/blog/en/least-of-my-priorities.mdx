## A proverb taken literally

"Love is blind" usually means you can't see a lover's flaws. Fujimoto's one-shot twists the proverb: Ibuki is blind to **everything except** love. Graduation duties, his own safety, the survival of the species: all of it drops out of focus.

This literalism is a comedy engine. It also hides a quiet argument about what matters.

## The ranking is the story

Read the story as a list:

1. Say the thing.
2. ~~The graduation ceremony.~~
3. ~~Getting robbed in the rain.~~
4. ~~The planet.~~

Each scene adds an item and puts it *below* the confession. Reviewers single out Ibuki's refrain because it is the story's thesis said out loud.

<SpeechBubble cite="Ibuki (VIZ edition, as quoted in reviews)" shout>
  “Planetary destruction is the least of my priorities!”
</SpeechBubble>

The Anitay review observes that the line lands because Ibuki *means it*. That's the key. Parody would wink. Fujimoto never lets Ibuki wink.

## Sincerity as the only weapon

Most stories that pit a teenager against an alien invasion hand them a power, a mecha or a plan. Ibuki has a sentence. The story suggests, half-jokingly and half not, that **saying the true thing** might be stronger than any of them. Anitay reads it as a statement about sincerity and the human spirit holding off disaster.

## *Sekai-kei* without the tears

Japanese fans have called *Love is Blind* a "new-generation *sekai-kei* gag comedy". *Sekai-kei* stories tie a small relationship to the fate of the world, typically with melancholy. Fujimoto keeps the structure and swaps the melancholy for slapstick. The world's fate *is* tied to one confession. It's just funny instead of tragic.

## The melancholy is still there

Under the gags runs a real ache: **he is leaving**. This walk home is the last one. The rain, the deadline and the sense that the moment will never come again are the same materials Fujimoto later uses for heartbreak. Here they become a punchline, but they aren't fake. That's why this site's palette is monochrome rain with one red heart. The comedy is loud, and the colour is the feeling underneath it.

## Why it still matters

Fujimoto went on to write stories where catastrophe usually wins. *Love is Blind* is the rare one where a feeling wins outright. Read it as the hopeful first draft of an idea his later work keeps testing: can one person's sincerity outweigh the world?
