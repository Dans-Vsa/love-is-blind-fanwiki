*Comparison essay. No major spoilers for any work; premises only.*

## The debut as a blueprint

Debuts are often read backwards, through what the author became. That's a trap, but with Fujimoto it's hard to resist. *Love is Blind* (drawn at 21, printed in 2014) already contains habits he never dropped.

| Habit | In *Love is Blind* | Later echo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tiny feeling vs. cosmic threat | A confession outranks an alien invasion | *Chainsaw Man*'s ordinary wants amid apocalyptic devils |
| Sudden genre whiplash | School rom-com → crime → sci-fi in a few pages | Most of *17-21* and *22-26* |
| Blunt sincerity | A confession said out loud, no speech prepared | Characters in *Look Back* and *Goodbye, Eri* stating feelings plainly |
| Cinematic cutting | Abrupt cuts to new threats | Film-influenced paneling across his serials |

*(The "later echo" column is my interpretation, not an official statement.)*

## Versus *Sasaki Stopped a Bullet* (2013)

Its neighbour in *17-21* is another schoolboy story with an outsized emotional ending. Fujimoto has reportedly called *Sasaki* his favourite of his own early one-shots. Both stories share the logic of **conviction bending reality**. *Love is Blind* is the looser, sillier sibling. The two were adapted back to back by the same studio (Lapin Track) in the 2025 anime, and they make a natural double bill.

## Versus *Fire Punch* (2016–18)

*Fire Punch* is everything *Love is Blind* isn't: bleak, violent, nihilistic. Yet both put an individual's will up against a world-ending situation. In the debut, the will wins in a single panel. In *Fire Punch*, the will survives at a terrible cost. Same equation, opposite sign.

## Versus *Chainsaw Man* (2018–2026)

The obvious comparison. Denji's famous small dreams (food, a girlfriend, a normal life) sit inside a world of devils. Ibuki's single dream sits inside an alien invasion. Both stories find comedy and pathos in a protagonist whose priorities are **wildly out of proportion** to the danger around him.

## Versus *Look Back* (2021) and *Goodbye, Eri* (2022)

These later one-shots are quieter and more autobiographical. They are about making art, grief and memory. They share *Love is Blind*'s faith that **saying or showing a feeling** matters. But where the debut plays it for laughs, *Look Back* plays it for tears. Reading them together is the best argument that Fujimoto's range was always about tone, not subject.

## Versus the rest of *17-21*

| Story | Year | Mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Couple Clucking Chickens… | 2011 | Sci-fi / allegory |
| Sasaki Stopped a Bullet | 2013 | School drama, surreal climax |
| **Love is Blind** | 2013–14 | Gag rom-com, sci-fi escalation |
| Shikaku | 2014 | Action / romance |

Within the book, *Love is Blind* is the comic relief and arguably the most cleanly constructed. The Comics Journal calls it probably the best-written piece in the volume.

## Conclusion

If *Chainsaw Man* is Fujimoto at full volume, *Love is Blind* is the same song hummed by a 21-year-old in the rain. The melody is there: a small human feeling, a ridiculous apocalypse, and a refusal to rank them the way the world expects.
