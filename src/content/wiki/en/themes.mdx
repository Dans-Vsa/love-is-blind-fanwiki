## 1. Priorities: the whole story in one line

The engine of *Love is Blind* is a single, absurd ranking: **one confession outranks the end of the world.** Ibuki's refrain sets out that ranking again and again, and reviewers single it out. Anitay argues the line hits hard *because he means it*. The joke never turns into irony.

<SpeechBubble cite="Ibuki (VIZ English edition, as quoted in reviews)" shout>
  “Planetary destruction is the least of my priorities!”
</SpeechBubble>

**Fan analysis:** The title is a well-known proverb, "love is blind". The proverb usually means love blinds you to a partner's faults. Fujimoto turns it into a blindness to *everything else*: duty, danger, the apocalypse. The comedy comes from taking the proverb literally.

## 2. Sincerity as a superpower

Anitay reads the story as a demonstration that sincerity and the human spirit can hold off disaster. Nothing defeats the obstacles except saying the true thing out loud. No fighting, no cleverness.

## 3. *Sekai-kei* turned into a gag

A Japanese fan review on Manba calls it a "new-generation *sekai-kei* gag comedy". *Sekai-kei* stories link a small personal relationship directly to the fate of the world, usually as tragedy. *Love is Blind* uses the same link as a punchline.

## 4. Momentum over economy

The Crown award judge, Takeshi Konomi, praised the story's ability to carry the reader to the end on sheer momentum. He also noted that it could have been told in half the pages. Fujimoto's afterword in *17-21* repeats the editors' complaint about his page count. **Fan analysis:** That "too many pages" quality is part of why the gag works. The long build-ups make the payoffs land harder.

## 5. Seeds of later Fujimoto

**Fan analysis (spoiler-light):** Several things here come back, much darker, in Fujimoto's later work:

- **Ordinary feeling against extraordinary catastrophe.** The tension between private emotion and world-scale violence runs through *Fire Punch* and *Chainsaw Man*.
- **Genre whiplash.** The story jumps from school romance to crime to sci-fi within a few pages. That is a trick Fujimoto keeps using.
- **Bluntly sincere confessions.** These come up throughout his short stories.
